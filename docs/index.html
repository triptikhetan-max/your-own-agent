Your own AI agent

A helper that never sleeps, remembers things, and texts you on your phone. I built one in April 2026 and people keep asking how. This walk takes about fifteen minutes to read and one evening to do.

It changes based on what you tell me, so first, three quick questions. There are no wrong answers.

1. Have you used ChatGPT or Claude?

2. Do you know what a "server" is?

3. Would you rather

First, the picture

What you are building

Three parts. Your phone, a small computer that never turns off, and a folder of text files. The text files are the important part. They say what your helper is called, how it talks, what it may never do, and what it does every morning. You write them in plain English.

Everything else is plumbing. If the small computer breaks, you rent a new one and copy the folder over. Same helper.

Any word with a dotted line under it, like , can be tapped for a one-sentence explanation. Nothing opens in a new page.

One real example before you build

What mine sent this morning

This is the text my agent sent me one Monday at 7am, with every name, place, and link taken out. Read it once. Everything on this page exists to get you to your own version of this.

☀️ Monday, the brief 📅 afternoon class, room not posted yet, watch the course chat 📞 10:00 call, you call them, prep notes ready ⏰ due in 48h: reflection poll for Tuesday's class 📝 waiting on one person: a proposal, promised for Sunday 📝 waiting on another: answers to my questions, Monday 📡 an application I wanted opens today 📡 lunch with the faculty advisors 📚 prep pack ready for tonight's class 🩺 all jobs healthy

And here is the whole routine that produced it, in the plain words I gave my helper. Five lines. The agent never looks at anything it was not pointed at.

## Morning brief - When: 7:00, my timezone, every day - Do: read my calendar feed, my deadlines file, and my "waiting on" list - Send: one Telegram message, one line per item, emoji first, under 15 lines - Last step: write today's date to state/last-routine.txt

The first version of this, in April, was three lines and read one calendar. The "waiting on" list and the health line came months later, one at a time. Start with three lines.

Where it lives

A computer that never sleeps

You rent a . It is a small computer in a building full of them, and it stays on for you. Two companies I trust for this, both about the price of a coffee a month.

Make an account, pick the smallest computer with on it, and when it asks for an , ask your helper: "help me make an SSH key for a new server." It will. Write down the address the company gives you. It looks like four numbers with dots.

You use your own Mac. Nothing to rent, nothing to pay. The trade: the helper only works while the lid is open. Close it and the helper sleeps too. If your one job is "when I open my laptop, tell me what changed," this is enough. If it must text you at 7 in the morning, it is not.

You can always move to a rented computer later. The folder of files comes with you, and the helper is the same helper.

You decide: if you are on the free route, tell your helper "I want to run this on my own Mac, no server" in the next step. It will follow a shorter path.

The thing that costs money

Get a brain key

Your agent needs an AI brain to think with. The company that runs the brain gives you a long password, the . Every time your agent thinks, it shows the key, and the company charges a fraction of a cent. That is the whole business model.

  1. Make the account and add a card.
  2. Find "limits" or "budget" and set $20 a month. Do this first.
  3. Create a key. Keep it ready for the next ten minutes. Never paste it into a note, an email, or a chat with anyone, including the chat with your helper. Your helper will give you one line to run in a second black window where you type it in yourself, and nothing shows on screen.

The $20 Claude Pro plan includes Claude Code, and your helper and your chats share one usage allowance, which is plenty for a setup evening. A subscription to ChatGPT or Claude is a different thing from a brain key. The subscription is for you to chat. The key is for your agent to think on its own. You may end up with both.

Its phone number

Make the Telegram bot

Install on your phone if you do not have it. Then, inside Telegram, find a contact called @BotFather. It is the official place bots are made. Send it the word /newbot. It asks for a name (anything) and a username (must end in "bot"). Then it gives you a long code. That code is a password too. Keep it ready; your helper will tell you exactly where to type it, and it is not the chat.

One more: message @userinfobot and it replies with your Telegram user ID, a number like 12345678. It is not your phone number. Your helper needs it so only you can talk to your agent.

You decide: the bot's name. Mine is TripBot. Pick something you will not mind seeing at 7am.

Where it becomes yours

Answer the six questions

Near the end, your helper asks six questions and turns your answers into the six text files. This is the part that makes it your agent and not mine. Think about them now.

What is it called, and what is it like?

A name and one line. "Pip. Calm, dry, a little nosy."

How should it talk?

Three words for its voice, three things it must never sound like. "Short, warm, direct. Never like a press release, never preachy, never fake cheerful."

Who are you?

Ten lines. Where you are, what you do, how you like to be spoken to, what annoys you.

What may it never do?

The most important answer. Mine: never delete a file, never message anyone but me, never post anywhere, never touch money.

What may it do only if you say yes?

Sending anything. Writing outside its own folders. Spending anything.

What is its one job, and when?

Your one sentence from earlier, plus a time. "Every morning at 7, read my calendar and text me what is due in three days."

You decide: all six. Keep each short. Long answers make it slower and more expensive, not smarter.
The first text

Say hello

Open Telegram, find your bot, and send three messages, one at a time.

  1. "Who are you?" It should answer with the name and voice you gave it.
  2. "What may you never do?" It should recite your never list. If it waffles, tell your helper: "it did not answer from the files."
  3. "What do you know about me?" It should answer from your ten lines and nothing else.

If all three land, it is alive. Take a screenshot. You built an agent.

If one fails, go back to the black window and describe what happened in plain words. "I asked what it may never do and it gave a generic answer." Your helper will find it.

The part everyone skips

Its one job, and a pulse

Now your helper sets the alarm clock so the one job runs on its own. And one more thing that sounds boring and matters more than anything on this page: a .

Here is why. A job that quietly stops running looks exactly like a job with nothing to say. You would not notice for weeks. So the job's last step is always "write today's date in a small file," and a tiny checker reads that file once a day and texts you only if the date is old.

Set up the routine in ROUTINE.md as a scheduled automation on the cheapest model. Make it write a heartbeat file when it finishes. Install scripts/health-check.sh to run once a day and text me only if something is red. Then run the automation once now so I can see it work.

"Cheap model" matters. The AI companies sell a strong brain and a cheaper, faster one. The alarm-clock jobs only need the cheap one. Chatting can use the strong one.

Five doors

Lock it

Five things actually go wrong with these. Not a hundred. Here they are, each with its lock. Your helper checks all of them when you paste the sentence at the bottom.

  • It reads a page that tells it what to do. Websites and emails can contain hidden instructions. Lock: a short rules file it is not allowed to change, and no power to do damage.
  • Its front door is open to the internet. Lock: it only listens to itself. Your helper checks this.
  • A key ends up in a note. Lock: keys live in one place, and the daily checker looks for stray ones.
  • The bill. Lock: the cap you already set, and the cheap brain for scheduled jobs.
  • It tidies up your files. Lock: "never delete" in its rules. It renames and tells you instead.

Two lists to keep

Where to start, and what you never do

How far this goes depends on how much you feed it. Here is the honest ladder. Each rung is a week or two of it running clean before the next.

  1. Tonight: it texts you back in its own voice. That is the whole first evening.
  2. Day 1: one morning job. What is due, or what is in a folder. Read-only.
  3. Week 2: it reads a folder of your notes and answers from them. This is where it stops feeling like a chatbot.
  4. Month 1: it drafts things for you. Replies, summaries, posts. You send every one.
  5. Month 2 and on: you talk to it through the day and it files, reminds, and argues back. Mine is here. It still gets things wrong every day.

The ceiling: it will not run your life. It saves the ten minutes a day you spend finding dates and the hour a week you spend filing, and it is there at 4am when a thought will not let you sleep.

Things you never do

  • Never paste the brain key or the bot code into a note, an email, or a chat, including the chat with your helper. Type them only where your helper points you, in the second window.
  • Never give it your email password, your bank, or your calendar with write access. Add powers one at a time, after a week.
  • Never let it post, send, or reply on its own. It drafts. You send.
  • Never skip the monthly cap on the brain key.
  • Never open its front door to the internet, and never run it as the boss account. Your helper sets both; do not undo them to make an error go away.
  • Never share the folder with anyone. After a month it contains your life.
  • Never believe "done" without looking. It says done when it thinks it is done.
  • Never fix it by pasting commands from a stranger on the internet. Describe the problem to your helper instead.

A note from me, before you start

I am not IT support. I am a learning designer who got curious. If your agent breaks, I am not going to fix it for you. If a hacker gets in, that is between you and the hacker, and this page told you how to lock the doors.

What I can tell you is how I fix mine. I explain the problem to my helper the way I would explain it to a friend. Messy. Typos. A screenshot of the error. "it was working yesterday and now the telegram thing just spins and i didnt change anything i swear." That works better than any command I could give you.

If it gets it wrong, I tell it so and try again. It is not a magic wand. It needs to know what you actually want, and that takes a bit of back and forth. Three tries is normal. Ten is a sign to close the laptop and come back tomorrow.
